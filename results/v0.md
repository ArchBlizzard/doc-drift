## Observations that drove the next iteration

1. **The plain baseline fails the way practitioners fear.** It confidently
   confirmed the case_01 no-empty-cells claim from a 50-row sample
   ("No empty/null cells observed in the 50-row sample" → `holds`) while the
   full file contains 17 injected nulls — the hallucinated-confirmation failure
   mode, on record in `v1_per_claim.csv` (case_01/baseline/a_nulls). Overall it
   caught 6/23 planted violations and leaned on `unverifiable` for anything the
   head sample could not settle (recall 0.26 on violations).
2. **baseline_plus is far stronger than sketched.** Full-column summary
   statistics settle 22 of 23 planted violations: ranges, counts, category
   sets, and means are all visible in `describe()`/value-counts. Its one miss
   is case_01 `a_nulls` — real NaNs hiding behind `?` sentinels — a claim only
   full-file execution settles.
3. **Consequence:** the 6-case suite is nearly saturated by a strong single
   prompt. The eval, not the agent, is the bottleneck — cases 07–12 (second
   corruption draws + the 1M-row hard case whose violations are invisible to
   summary statistics) carry the burden of separating execution from
   summary-reading.
